Read the bill before you pay it.

Second Opinion goes through an itemized hospital bill line by line, explains each charge in plain English, and marks the few lines worth asking about. Then it drafts a calm letter to the billing office.

Three kinds of question

  • Numbers disagreeTwo figures on your own papers don't match.
  • Worth askingIt may have an ordinary explanation. It's worth a question.
  • Ask for detailThe bill doesn't say enough to tell.

The level is always written in words. The shape of the mark says the same thing.

Choose a tag to pull its lines out of the statement.

A high price is not a finding.

Second Opinion never calls a charge wrong because it is large. It looks for things you can check against your own papers: totals that don't add up, a line printed twice, a date outside your stay.

Every question comes with the ordinary explanation beside it. Most are settled with one phone call, and the letter only asks.

It reads a CSV file or lines you type in. It does not read PDFs or photos. It drafts the letter, and you decide whether to send it.
